    ADR-013: Whole-shoot plant-nitrogen accounting (critical-N dilution)¶

    Status¶

    Accepted (2026-07-03) — #360 (follow-up to #351 / #357).

    Amendment 2026-09-07 — uptake is demand-driven; mass flow is a diagnostic¶

    Decision point 3 described soil N uptake as "mass-flow (soil-supply) limited". The uptake in NitrogenCycle._take_up_plant is demand-driven and capped by the mineral N available in each rooted layer. The transpiration mass-flow term is computed for diagnosis only and published as MassFlowNSupplyComputed(total_kg_ha, by_layer); it neither debits the nitrate pool nor credits the plant stock. Debiting it removed nitrate that no plant stock ever received (of the order of 100 kg N/ha per season under a full maize canopy), and crediting it would double-count the demand-driven uptake.

    Amended 2026-11-25 (#694, below): uptake is capped by the mineral N in each rooted layer above a residual, below which roots cannot extract it.

    Amendment 2026-11-18 — no N uptake before emergence; no demand floor (#578)¶

    Decision point 4 makes the N demand the deficit to critical, and the Persistence section has a new crop start with ~0 shoot N. The orchestrator nonetheless returned max(0.1, deficit), an uncited floor that took 0.1 kg N/ha/day from the soil from the sowing day. The stock banked 2-3 kg N/ha behind the 1.0 NNI sentinel before emergence, and on the first seedling day it divided by a near-zero shoot DM: NNI 9.7 under the legacy Netherlands weather and up to 39 under De Bilt station normals.

    The demand for a planted crop is now exactly the deficit to critical, with no floor. It is 0 while shoot DM is at or below 1 kg/ha (before emergence) and 0 whenever the stock is at or above critical, so no soil N is taken up before emergence. This follows CERES-Maize, where DSSAT-CSM MZ_CERES.for runs growth and N uptake only for ISTAGE 1-6, emergence to maturity (Jones et al. 2003, Eur. J. Agron. 18:235-265), and APSIM Next Generation PMF, whose OrganArbitrator estimates and sets N uptake only when plant.IsEmerged (Brown et al. 2014, Environ. Model. Softw. 62:385-398). Neither model has a demand floor.

    Amendment 2026-11-24 — no N uptake after maturity (#539)¶

    The post-maturity counterpart of the #578 amendment. Shoot DM is frozen at MATURITY (#433), so the critical-N target is fixed, and a crop standing below critical kept filling its stock to that target: Kenya maize gained about 85 kg N/ha over 300 standing days on top of the 94 it took up in the season, and scavenged every kilogram the soil mineralised meanwhile. Under #531 a mature crop stands until the player harvests it, so this reached play.

    The N demand is now 0 while the phenology stage is MATURITY, so the stock holds at its maturity value. CERES-Maize runs growth and uptake only for ISTAGE 1-6, setting UNO3 = UNH4 = PUptake = 0 otherwise, and ISTAGE 6 itself returns with no N or K uptake (DSSAT-CSM MZ_CERES.for:665-740, MZ_GROSUB.for:1621-1623; Jones et al. 2003, Eur. J. Agron. 18:235-265). The WOFOST crop parameter files define DVS_NPK_STOP, the development stage above which no crop N/P/K uptake occurs (de Wit et al. 2019, Agric. Syst. 168:154-167); PCSE declares the parameter but does not apply it, so it is cited as the WOFOST definition, not as PCSE behaviour.

    P, S, Fe, Zn and Mn uptake stop on the same rule: P and S demand are 0 at MATURITY, ahead of their 0.01 and 0.005 kg/ha/day floors, and the micronutrient pools equilibrate as on a fallow patch (#542). Before this they kept drawing on the last grain-fill day's growth increment, which no longer updates once growth stops. Water uptake is not gated: transpiration follows green LAI through the late season, as the FAO-56 late-season crop coefficient declines to Kc_end without a step to zero (Allen et al. 1998, Table 12). The root front stops at MATURITY too, and root turnover continues (APSIM Next Generation Root.cs). Every gate reads the saved phenology stage, so no runtime memory is added (#585). The /forecast mineral-N projection drops its crop-uptake sink for a mature anchor.

    Superseded by the 2026-11-25 amendment (#623): the sentence "Water uptake is not gated: transpiration follows green LAI through the late season, as the FAO-56 late-season crop coefficient declines to Kc_end without a step to zero" no longer holds after maturity. A determinate crop now has no green leaf from its first MATURITY step.

    Amendment 2026-11-25 — no green leaf after maturity (#623)¶

    This replaces the 2026-11-24 water sentence. The FAO-56 late-season decline to Kc_end describes the crop before harvest; it does not license the green canopy that kept transpiring after physiological maturity, where green LAI decayed only at the peak grain-fill senescence rate (e-folding 50 days for maize, 62 for spring wheat). A standing NL spring wheat crop transpired 91 mm in the 60 days after maturity, against 127 mm in its season, and standing Sahel maize and sorghum 95 and 102 mm (medians, seeds 42-51).

    A determinate crop (maize, wheat, sorghum, rice, soybean) now senesces all green leaf at its first MATURITY canopy step, so LAI is 0.0 from that day and transpiration, rain interception and the client follow it with no separate gate. WOFOST ends the crop at DVSEND by default and nothing transpires after (crop_finish; PCSE phenology.py:473-476, engine.py:330-333, classic_waterbalance.py:421-422); DSSAT-CSM harvests at maturity unless the experiment file says otherwise (IHARI = 'M'; AUTHAR.for:152-154, IPSIM.for:155). Where the crop stands, CERES-Wheat senesces the last green leaf to zero at LSPHE 6.3, 60 °C·d after maturity (CER_Growth.for:818-822, WHCER048.ECO/.SPE), and FAO-56 gives field-dried grain maize a Kcb_end of 0.15, which is Kc_min for dry bare soil (Allen et al. 1998, Tables 12 and 17). Grape is exempt through the preset flag green_leaf_dies_at_maturity: its MATURITY is fruit ripeness on a vine that still transpires. The FAO-56 grape rows (table/raisin and wine) end the season at Kc_end 0.45 (Table 12) and Kcb_end 0.40 (Table 17), above Kc_min 0.15–0.20 for dry bare soil (Eq. 76). Table 12 footnote 18, which gives Kc_end prior to leaf drop, sits on the deciduous fruit- and nut-tree rows, not on grapes, and is an analogy only.

    The gate reads the saved phenology stage, so a save taken after maturity before this change loses its green leaf at the first step after loading, and no runtime memory is added (#585). The canopy phase runs after et (ADR-010), so the season to maturity is unchanged. One consequence was accepted with the ruling: the standing crop no longer dries the profile, so a resown Sahel crop starts on stored subsoil water (resown maize grain about +60% median, sorghum about +12%); #662 checks that carry-over against the model's dry-season soil evaporation.

    Amendment 2026-11-25 — uptake stops at a residual (#694)¶

    Decision point 3 and the 2026-09-07 amendment now read: soil N uptake is demand-driven and capped by the mineral N in each rooted layer above a residual of 0.25 mg/kg NO3-N and 0.5 mg/kg NH4-N (CERES MZ_NUPTAK XMIN), below which roots cannot extract it. Edwin accepted this on 2026-11-25 (Option 1 of the

    694 refinement).¶

    Before this, _take_up_plant removed min(pool, share) down to an empty pool, and the demand is the whole deficit to critical (decision point 4), so unfertilised maize drained the whole profile to 0.2-0.6 kg N/ha from day 38-86 (by soil) to maturity (seeds 42-51) and NNI settled wherever the day's mineralisation put it. CERES-Maize caps each layer's uptake at SNO3 - 0.25/KG2PPM and SNH4 - 0.5/KG2PPM, with KG2PPM = 10/(BD*DLAYR) (DSSAT-CSM v4.8.6.0 Plant/CERES-Maize/MZ_NUPTAK.for:211-214; Jones et al. 2003, Eur. J. Agron. 18:235-265). CERES-Sorghum (SG_NUPTAK.for:207-210) and CROPGRO (Plant/CROPGRO/NUPTAK.for:170-177) use the same values, and CERES-Wheat documents both as its original XMIN (Plant/CERES-Wheat_Barley/CER_Growth.for:1344, 1348). In kg N/ha per layer the residual is 0.25 × BD × depth_cm / 10 (NO3) and 0.5 × BD × depth_cm / 10 (NH4), 11-14 kg N/ha over the 1 m presets.

    • Only uptake is floored. Leaching, denitrification, nitrification and volatilisation are not, as in DSSAT, where XMIN appears only in the uptake routine.
    • Above the residual, uptake is unchanged: demand-driven, split by root fraction, NO3 before NH4, with no redistribution of a layer's shortfall. CERES's first-order concentration and soil-moisture terms are not adopted; the refinement found the first-order term non-binding on these soils and the moisture term crop-specific across the DSSAT families.
    • Consequences, measured at seeds 42-51: standing-season Nmin on unfertilised NL and Kenya maize rises from 0.2-0.6 to 9.8-10.4 kg N/ha; unfertilised grain falls 3-24 % (the crop no longer mines the last ~10 kg N/ha); fertilised grain is unchanged within 2 %. The residual carried past maturity is exposed to winter leaching, which leaves its nitrification H+ (ADR-020), so the five-year 0 kg N pH drift of ADR-020 A4(b) is 0.011; its test tolerance moved from 0.01 to 0.02 with the same ruling.
    • No state or runtime memory is added (#585): the residual is computed from the profile when the cycle is built. A save taken with pools below the residual simply pauses uptake until mineralisation lifts them above it.

    Context¶

    Before this change the crop's N-stress signal was flow-based: the nitrogen runtime compared the day's soil N uptake against the day's demand (clamp(uptake / demand, 0..1)) and fed that ratio into the canopy's Liebig min() on RUE. Because same-day demand is small and usually met, the ratio sat near 1 (satisfied) or collapsed to near 0 (starved), giving a bimodal on/off response with little middle ground. A realistic, high-magnitude, graded response to fertiliser rate needs a stock of plant N compared against a critical-N dilution curve, as in DSSAT (CERES) and APSIM.

    Decision¶

    1. Whole-shoot, not per-organ. Track a single whole-shoot N stock (kg/ha) rather than per-organ (leaf/stem/grain) pools. This is the Greenwood / Justes / Plénet–Lemaire critical-N framework and keeps the scope at L. Per-organ accounting is a possible future refinement.

    2. New agrogame/plant/nitrogen/ package. The stock, the critical-N curve, the NNI, and the NNI→stress mapping live in a dedicated plant-side package (params.py / state.py / module.py / runtime.py) rather than being bolted onto NitrogenCycle/NitrogenRuntime. The nitrogen cycle is already the #3 "god node" in the dependency graph; adding plant-physiology logic there would deepen the coupling. The change is additive: a new layer that consumes the existing soil N uptake.

    3. Uptake unchanged; stress derived from the stock. Soil N uptake stays mass-flow (soil-supply) limited in NitrogenCycle._take_up_plant — we do not change how uptake is limited. The nitrogen runtime stops emitting the flow-based NutrientStressComputed and instead emits PlantNUptakeComputed(uptake, demand). PlantNitrogenRuntime consumes it, accumulates the stock, computes the N nutrition index, and emits the single graded NutrientStressComputed(N) the canopy reads. (Uptake limitation amended 2026-09-07 and 2026-11-25; see the amendments above.)

    4. Stock-based N demand. The N demand handed to the nitrogen cycle is now the deficit between the stock and the critical-N target for the current shoot DM (DSSAT/APSIM crop N demand), replacing the legacy same-day biomass_increment × tissue_conc × 0.5 formula for N. Without this, the 0.5 same-day-remobilisation cap held cumulative uptake at ~half the crop's need, so the stock could never approach critical N and NNI was pinned low regardless of fertiliser. P demand keeps its own formula.

    5. Liebig min() retained at the canopy. Gradedness comes from NNI being continuous, not from switching the canopy to a multiplicative combiner.

    6. Cross-domain event placement. PlantNUptakeComputed lives in agrogame.plant.events alongside NutrientStressComputed, so the soil → plant import (soil.nitrogen.runtime → plant.events) reuses the already-whitelisted event-subscription edge (ADR-008); the plant layer never imports soil.nitrogen.

    Model¶

    For shoot dry matter W (t/ha) and stock N_stock (kg/ha):

    • Critical N: N_crit% = a · W^-b, held flat below a reference biomass (~1 t/ha) to avoid the W → 0 divergence. Coefficients:
    • maize 3.40 · W^-0.37 — Plénet & Lemaire (2000), Plant & Soil 216:65–82 (orig. 1999).
    • wheat 5.35 · W^-0.442 — Justes et al. (1994), Ann. Bot. 74:397–407.
    • fallback (crops without a fitted curve) 5.70 · W^-0.50 — the generic C3 dilution of Greenwood et al. (1990), Ann. Bot. 66:425–436.
    • Amendment 2026-11-24 (#603): the flat-below threshold is per crop. Justes et al. (1994), Ann. Bot. 74:397-407, state the wheat law holds between 1.55 and 12 t/ha and give a constant N_ct = 4.4 % below 1.55 t/ha; 5.35 · 1.55^-0.442 = 4.408 %, so the curve is continuous there and the existing max(W, reference) form reproduces the cited constant with reference_biomass_t_ha = 1.55 (preset key n_crit_reference_biomass_t_ha). Wheat uses 1.55; every other crop keeps 1.0 (the Greenwood et al. 1990 domain, W > 1 t/ha). Previously wheat was held at 5.35 % below 1 t/ha, 21 % above the cited constant.
    • Actual N: N_actual% = 100 · N_stock / (W·1000).
    • NNI: N_actual% / N_crit% (Lemaire & Gastal 1997).
    • Stress: a documented linear rescale of NNI between nni_stress_min and nni_stress_ref, clamped to [stress_floor, 1]. Luxury uptake (NNI > 1) is capped at 1.0 (no growth bonus). The defaults (min=0, ref=1, floor=0.05) give clamp(NNI, 0.05..1) with the luxury cap; the anchors exist as a CERES-Maize-NFAC-style calibration lever (Jones et al. 2003).

    Persistence¶

    The whole-shoot N stock is a within-season plant property and is intentionally not persisted in SoilSnapshot (which captures soil pools only). A new crop starts with ~0 shoot N; reset_crop rebuilds plant_n_state fresh. This is covered by a two-cycle test.

    Consequences¶

    • Fertiliser dose-response over 0–240 kg N/ha is monotone, smooth and saturating (Mitscherlich-type), with the agronomic knee (~90% of max) around 120–160 kg N/ha on the N-depleted Kenya-highlands maize scenario.
    • The N-demand trajectory front-loads (building the stock during establishment/grand growth, then tapering) rather than tracking the daily biomass increment; the demand-trajectory realism test was updated to match.
    • All existing realism bands (NL/Kenya/Sahel, fertilized > 3× unfertilized) stay green.
