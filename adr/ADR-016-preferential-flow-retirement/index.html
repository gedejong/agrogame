    ADR-016: Preferential flow is structural, not hydrological (dual-porosity model retired)¶

    Status¶

    Accepted — decided 2026-10-02 (Option B of #466), implemented by #466. Amends ADR-010. Expected to be superseded by the ADR that #485 (Preferential flow v2) will require.

    Context¶

    213 delivered DualPorosityWaterModel and DualPorosityParams¶

    (agrogame/soil/water/models/dual_porosity.py), a first-order exchange helper (dual_porosity_exchange.py) and a PreferentialFlowOccurred event, as part of the pore-network umbrella #74. The model was MACRO/RZWQM-style. It split rainfall between the matrix and a macropore domain by rainfall intensity against topsoil Ksat, routed the bypass share through macropore storage, and returned it to the matrix through a first-order exchange term (Jarvis 2007; Gerke & van Genuchten 1993; Ahuja et al. 2000). It carried unit and realism tests.

    The model never ran in the simulation. FullSimulationOrchestrator builds CascadingBucketWaterModel unconditionally, and nothing in agrogame/ constructed the dual-porosity model. ADR-010 recorded the wiring as deferred, but its Consequences section still claimed that heavy-rain bypass surfaced in simulation outputs. That was false. #466 asked to wire the model in or retire it.

    The pore chain reaches the water balance through WaterRuntime, but not as preferential flow. The Ksat factor is driven by aggregation (agg_state.macro), not by biopores. Porosity comes from effective_porosity(), which is total porosity minus cryptoporosity (#289). Biopore donation takes from crypto first, so it can raise that cap by at most the biopore volume fraction, about 0.0015 m³/m³ at 500 pores/m². That made no measurable difference. On clay_loam_temperate and clay_temperate, ten days of 40 mm/day gave the same deep drainage and profile water to 1e-6 mm at 0, 80 and 500 pores/m², measured at bb276b4.

    Decision¶

    Preferential flow is modelled structurally but not hydrologically.

    • Structural (kept). The pore network (PoreNetworkModule) partitions porosity into macro, meso, micro and crypto pools. Biopores (ADR-009) form from root turnover, decay over time, are destroyed by tillage, and donate their volume into the macropore pool. This structure is computed, persisted and reported by the API, but no flux reads the partition. Gas diffusion (ADR-010) uses total porosity, which the partition and the donation conserve, so neither macropores nor biopores change aeration, O₂ or Eh. The only partition-sensitive reader is the effective-porosity cap described under Context, which made no measurable difference. That was measured untilled (10 × 40 mm/day). After a moldboard pass, 10 × 80 mm at 500 /m² moves θ by ≤ 5.5e-4 m³/m³ and NO₃ leaching by ≤ 0.23 kg/ha, about 100× less at in-game densities (#524, ADR-010).
    • Hydrological (not modelled). There is no infiltration bypass, no fast drainage path to depth, and no macropore solute transport. Water and nitrate move only through the cascading bucket and its WaterDrained events.

    Removed in #466:

    • agrogame/soil/water/models/dual_porosity.py (DualPorosityParams, DualPorosityWaterModel, partition_flow) and agrogame/soil/water/models/dual_porosity_exchange.py, together with their re-exports from agrogame.soil.water.
    • The PreferentialFlowOccurred event.
    • The macropore water domain: SoilWaterState.theta_macro and SoilWaterState.enable_dual_porosity().
    • The SoilSnapshot.water_theta_macro field. It is no longer written. SoilSnapshot.from_dict reads keys explicitly, so older saves that still contain the key load and ignore it (ADR-001). This is pinned by tests/test_save_load.py::TestRetiredMacroDomainSnapshotField.
    • tests/test_dual_porosity.py and the realism tests test_dual_porosity_heavy_rain_bypass and test_dual_porosity_light_rain_no_bypass. These are the only test deletions authorised. test_heavy_rain_on_clay_bypass_visible_in_pore_chain, which asserts only pore-chain bounds, was renamed to test_heavy_rain_on_clay_keeps_pore_chain_bounds_coherent with its assertions unchanged.

    Kept: DailyDrivers.rainfall_intensity_mm_hr. It has no consumer now, but it is the input #485 needs.

    Why: three findings¶

    Do not re-implement the retired model as it stood. Wiring it in would not have produced preferential flow, for three reasons.

    The #466 refinement measured each model on a fresh orchestrator's initialised profile and pore state, for one day with no evaporation, starting from identical copies of the water state. The table reproduces exactly at bb276b4 (depths in mm):

    preset, rain intensity cascading deep / runoff dual-porosity deep / runoff
    clay_temperate, 40 mm None (production path) 19.6 / 20.4 19.6 / 20.4
    clay_temperate, 40 mm 10 mm/h 19.6 / 20.4 8.9 / 0.7
    clay_temperate, 80 mm 20 mm/h 23.8 / 56.2 18.1 / 0.7
    loam_temperate, 60 mm 15 mm/h 31.3 / 28.7 24.9 / 11.1
    1. The model was inert on the production path. Nothing sets DailyDrivers.rainfall_intensity_mm_hr, so intensity fell back to rain / 24. Bypass needed intensity above 0.6 × Ksat_top (the bypass_threshold_factor, Jarvis 2007, 0.5 to 0.8). That threshold is 57.6 mm/day on clay_temperate (Ksat_top 4 mm/h) and 72 mm/day on clay_netherlands (5 mm/h). Below those amounts the two models give identical results (row 1). A sub-daily intensity disaggregation is new science that needs its own citation. The SWAT α₀.₅ half-hour peak fraction (Neitsch et al. 2011, SWAT Theoretical Documentation, §2:1.3) is a candidate.
    2. When it fired, deep drainage fell instead of rising. The routing filled macropore storage top-down from layer 0, which holds about 61 mm on clay_temperate. The first-order exchange (α_w = 0.2 d⁻¹) then returned the water to the matrix. Water parked near the surface instead of bypassing to depth. Deep drainage dropped from 19.6 to 8.9 mm, from 23.8 to 18.1 mm, and from 31.3 to 24.9 mm (rows 2 to 4). The fall in runoff is physically plausible. The fall in drainage is the opposite of what macropore flow does in structured clay (Beven & Germann 1982; Jarvis 2007).
    3. Bypass water carried no nitrate. Macropore routing and exchange emitted no WaterDrained. NitrogenCycle._on_water_drained is the only NO₃ transport path, so rain diverted from the matrix lowered nitrate leaching. Macropore solute transport would be needed, for example MACRO's mixing-depth inflow concentration (Larsbo & Jarvis 2003, MACRO 5.0 technical description). That is new science too.

    Wiring the model in as it stood would therefore have forced a recalibration of water-balance and leaching bounds (for example test_seasonal_no3_leaching_contrast_and_band and the Sahel bound relaxed in

    458) and of clay-preset yields. That work would have served a model whose¶

    drainage and leaching move in the wrong direction.

    Approvals¶

    All three come from Edwin de Jong, 2026-10-02, relayed via the manager session and recorded in #466:

    • Option B (retire) was chosen over Option A (wire in and recalibrate).
    • Deleting the tests listed above was explicitly authorised.
    • This ADR was explicitly approved.

    The #466 PR carries a merge hold: Edwin merges it, not an agent.

    Path back¶

    The capability continues as #485 (Preferential flow v2), phase 3 of the roadmap in #476. Its acceptance criteria are the three findings above:

    1. sub-daily rainfall intensity;
    2. routing that bypasses to depth;
    3. macropore solute transport.

    The recalibration and a new ADR superseding this one come after those. The retired code can be recovered for reference with git show bb276b4:agrogame/soil/water/models/dual_porosity.py. Do not restore it as-is, for the reasons above.

    Amendments to ADR-010¶

    • Context: the #213 entry is marked as retired, and the "wiring is intentionally deferred" sentence now points here.
    • Consequences → Easier: the "realism follows physics" bullet no longer claims that heavy-rain bypass surfaces in simulation outputs. The snapshot bullet no longer lists water_theta_macro.
    • Consequences → Harder and Follow-ups: the "DualPorosity is still not opt-in" item and the "DualPorosity config flag" follow-up are replaced by a pointer to this ADR and #485.

    Consequences¶

    Easier¶

    • No module, event or ADR claims hydrological behaviour that the running simulation does not have.
    • One water model. Nothing in the water balance carries a dormant second domain.
    • The change is numerically inert. Two seasons of maize on clay_temperate and on loam_temperate, including reset_crop and a JSON save/restore between seasons, give identical yield, biomass, deep drainage, runoff and NO₃ leaching before and after (max |Δ| = 0 over every compared scalar, at a 1e-9 tolerance). No realism bound, crop parameter, preset or calibration value moved.

    Harder¶

    • Clay presets under-represent rapid drainage and nitrate bypass after heavy storms on structured soil. Outside saturated layers, biopores have no measurable consequence for water, aeration or rooting; they change only the reported pore-network partition (see Decision). The hydrological gap is tracked in #485.
    • DailyDrivers.rainfall_intensity_mm_hr is accepted but read by nothing until #485 lands.
    • Saves written before #466 carry an ignored water_theta_macro key. It is dropped on the next save.

    Alternatives Considered¶

    Option A: wire it in and recalibrate¶

    Rejected on the three findings above. Wiring alone changes nothing on the production path (finding 1). Once intensity exists, the model moves drainage and leaching in the wrong direction (findings 2 and 3). Fixing that is a multi-issue science programme, now #485, and not a wiring task.

    Keep the module dormant and fix only the documentation¶

    Rejected. Dormant code with literature citations, unit tests and a passing realism test looks like a quick win: "just wire it in". That is the trap this ADR records. The code would also keep a second water domain and a snapshot field alive for no running behaviour.

    Opt-in config flag (ADR-010's follow-up)¶

    Rejected for the same reason. A flag would offer players and calibration runs a mode whose drainage and leaching move in the wrong direction.
