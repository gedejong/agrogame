    ADR-022: Diorama presentation: the soil on the farm's cut edges, a world around the farm¶

    Status¶

    Accepted (Edwin, 2026-11-25), with three changes to the proposal: - assets are downloaded and realistic, not procedural low-poly (decision 5); - the slab is drawn deeper than the simulated profile, for its look (decision 1); - the shaded face gets a fill light (decision 1). Withdrawn on 2026-11-25 by the lighting amendment below.

    Amended (Edwin, 2026-11-25; #680): no fill on the shaded face, more sky ambient, and one cited soil colour for the tops and the faces (decision 1, Lighting).

    Edwin chose the direction on 2026-11-24 ("Yes to 1 and 2. Also, would be nice to have a bit of elevation").

    Amends ADR-005 ("The Underground", and its stylised art direction for world assets) and ADR-007 (item 4, soil cutaway).

    Context¶

    ADR-005 made the underground the game's differentiator and specified it as a mode: click a patch and the camera slides into a 3D cross-section. In the code on main, View Soil digs a pit into the field. It hides the three tiles in front of the selection, raises a soil pillar with a highlighted outline and glass flow tubes, and opens a Soil Analysis panel with fifteen readouts for each of three layers. Five files carry the mode: soil_view.gd (504 lines), soil_cutaway_controller.gd (265), flow_tube.gd (630), flow_overlay.gd (822) and soil_cutaway.gdshader (127), with their tests.

    Edwin's verdict after playing it (2026-11-24): he wanted only a bit of the soil underneath, as an impression, and the pit is too much and feels unnatural. He also wants the managed fields set in a landscape (neighbouring fields, trees, a barn), some elevation, and eventually the whole business of running a farm.

    The pit also carries a known defect: it draws roots at about twice the reported depth (#634). A cosmetic fix there would polish a mode this ADR removes.

    Decision¶

    1. The world is a diorama slab. The land is a finite slab, and its two camera-facing cut faces always show the soil beneath the surface above them. The managed fields sit at the front corner, so each front patch's own profile shows directly under it.
    2. Under a managed patch, the face is drawn from the simulation and updated each day tick. Horizon boundaries come from SoilProfile.layers. Each horizon darkens with its organic carbon and with its water content relative to field capacity. Root traces reach RootState.current_depth_cm, which the model caps at the profile bottom (#651), with density fading with depth.
    3. Depth (Edwin, 2026-11-25).
      • The simulated profile is drawn at true vertical scale, so roots and horizons keep their real proportions against the canopy.
      • Below it, the slab continues for its look, to 4 to 8 times the profile's depth. It shows realistic regional layers (subsoil, parent material, weathered rock), artefacts such as stones, old roots and gravel lenses, and surface relief through normal or bump maps.
      • The extension is decorative: no simulation value drives it.
    4. Lighting (amended 2026-11-25, #680). The faces and their decorative extension take the scene's light alone, from farm_environment.tres; the fill on the face turned from the sun is withdrawn. Edwin, on the slab with the fill: "I would expect the color of the 'top' of the soil (what you can normally see), to extend normally to the cutout, but now, due to the extra lighting, it seems unrealistic, especially on the left side (where it is in the shadow). I think we need a bit more overall ambient light, and it's ok to make the left side darker to fit the shadows."
      • The ambient rises instead, as a gain on the sky's radiance map (farm_sky.gdshader), so the background sky does not brighten. On a clear day the shaded face reads about 0.40 of the sunlit one in scene-linear light: the upper end of 0.26 to 0.42 for an isotropic sky (Liu & Jordan 1963) at a clear-day diffuse fraction of 0.165 to 0.24 (Erbs, Klein & Duffie 1982). The gain rises by a quarter at full overcast. The tone mapper stays Filmic, with its exposure re-balanced so the canopy's lightness holds (#637).
      • Amended 2026-11-25 (Edwin; #692): the background sky the camera sees returns to its brightness before #680. It alone regains the exposure #680 took away (1.40 / 0.89), its fog's share included; the radiance map, and so the light on the scene, is unchanged.
      • The top and the face's top band take one colour: the Munsell value of the cited mapping, and a cited Munsell hue and chroma per soil preset and layer. Soils differ as much as the cited ones do, and no more.
    5. Under decorative land, the face shows a static profile typical of the scenario's region.
    6. Soil detail follows the same rule: drawn from the simulation where the model holds the quantity, decorative only outside it.
      • Old root channels come from the biopore density per layer, the channels decayed roots leave (ADR-009).
      • Grey and rust redox mottles appear where a layer's redox potential drops into the iron-reducing range (Vepraskas 1992). They are the visible trace of a fluctuating water table.
      • Below the simulated profile, the decorative extension shows regional parent material: rock fragments, ironstone gravel or marine clay.
      • No aquifer or water table is drawn while the water balance drains freely and has no groundwater. Adding one is a hydrology decision, not a presentation one.
    7. ADR-005's goal 2 (showing what happens underground) stands. It is now delivered as an ambient, always-visible edge, not as a mode.
    8. The pit cutaway is retired. The pit, the hidden tiles, the pillar, the flow tubes and the cutaway shader go, with their tests; the tests cover only the retired code. View Soil opens the Soil Analysis panel, trimmed to a few headline values per layer, with the rest behind Expand.
    9. Surroundings. A ring of non-simulated land frames the managed fields:
    10. neighbouring fields in a regional rotation;
    11. hedgerows or tree rows, a ditch and a farm track;
    12. a farmyard with barn, farmhouse, silo and machine shed.

    The sets follow the scenario's climate key: a Dutch polder, Kenyan highland smallholdings, the Sahel. Surroundings show no simulation values and cannot be selected. Later farm-business epics may make them interactive (barn storage, renting a neighbouring field); each needs its own design. 4. Elevation. The surroundings get gentle, region-appropriate relief: a dike and a faint rise in the polder, hills in the Kenyan highlands, low dunes in the Sahel. The cut faces follow the terrain's top edge. - Managed fields stay level, because the water balance assumes level ground (the SCS curve number has no slope term). - Sloping a managed field would first need a slope-adjusted curve number (Williams 1995, as used in EPIC and SWAT) and erosion. That is a separate decision. 5. Assets are downloaded and realistic (Edwin, 2026-11-25: "not low-poly, you can download assets"). Buildings, trees, props, terrain and soil materials come from asset libraries as glTF models and PBR texture sets (albedo, normal, roughness), not from procedural low-poly meshes. This amends ADR-005's stylised art direction for world assets. - Licence. Use CC0 sources first, such as Poly Haven and ambientCG. Anything that needs attribution, or carries any other licence, needs Edwin's approval first. - Provenance. game/assets/CREDITS.md records, for every file: its source URL, author, licence and download date, plus any edits made. - Review. Binary assets can't be reviewed as text. Each asset PR therefore carries /visual-check frames for every scenario it touches, and a GUT test that checks the asset loads and has its credits entry. - Weight. Import at the smallest resolution that reads at default zoom. Each PR reports the bytes it adds. Git LFS (Edwin, 2026-11-25; #652): downloaded binaries under game/assets/materials/ and game/assets/models/ live in Git LFS; the textures already in Git stay there. CI jobs that load assets check out with lfs: true. Each asset PR reports its LFS bytes and the projected monthly CI download against the free 10 GiB; the PR that takes LFS past about 20 MB at HEAD proposes a CI cache. - Placement, variety and seeding stay procedural: the scenario's climate key and a deterministic seed choose and place assets. 6. Performance budget. At default zoom, the slab and surroundings together add at most 3 ms of GPU frame time over the baseline after #640, measured on the development machine. Realistic assets keep to it through levels of detail, instancing and texture sizes; a PR that cannot meet it reports why, and Edwin decides. Decorative crops use instanced meshes, not the managed-field crop renderer. 7. Framing. The default camera keeps the managed fields as the subject; zooming out reveals the surroundings.

    Consequences¶

    • About 2,350 lines of client code and their tests are removed. #634 closes as superseded, and so does the cutaway and flow-tube half of #641.
    • The soil is visible all the time, so weather and growth show up underground without the player opening anything.
    • Fewer layers are visible at once than in the pit, and decorative land gives no data. The Soil Analysis panel remains the place for detail.
    • New code and assets for the slab, terrain, vegetation and buildings need visual review for each scenario; /visual-check frames per climate become part of each PR.
    • Downloaded assets add binary weight and a licence duty; CREDITS.md and the load-and-credits test carry that duty.
    • Farm-business features (storage, land, machinery) get a physical home in the farmyard.

    Alternatives Considered¶

    • Keep the pit but declutter it. This keeps the mode Edwin finds unnatural and the hidden-tile machinery.
    • A 2D profile chart in a side panel. Clear, but it removes the soil from the world, and ADR-005's goal is that the player sees the soil as part of the land.
    • Procedural low-poly assets generated in GDScript. Reviewable as text and reproducible, but Edwin rejected the low-poly look on 2026-11-25.
    • The slab at the simulated depth only (1×) or exaggerated (2×). At 1× the face is about 31 px tall at 1280×720 and reads poorly; 2× doubles the roots against the canopy. Edwin chose true scale plus a decorative extension below.
    • Slope on managed fields now. It needs hydrology changes (above); this ADR keeps elevation visual.
