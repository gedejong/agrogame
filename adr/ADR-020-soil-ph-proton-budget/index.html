    ADR-020: Soil pH as an N-cycle proton budget buffered by pH buffer capacity¶

    Status¶

    Accepted — 2026-11-18.

    Edwin accepted the shared soil-pH design as proposed, decisions (a) to (d), on 2026-11-18 (ruling relayed on #481 and #492). This ADR records that design once for both issues:

    • (a) is implemented by #492, which drafts this ADR;
    • (b) by #481 PR A (implemented);
    • (c) and (d) by #481 PR B.

    481's PRs amend this ADR as they land, with the status of each part.¶

    Context¶

    ChemistryState.ph holds one pH (in water) per soil layer. It starts at ChemistryParams.base_ph = 6.8 on every preset and carries across reset_crop and saves through SoilSnapshot.ph. Nitrification, NH3 volatilisation, P availability and fixation, S sorption, Fe/Zn/Mn availability and microbial activity all read it through the SoilPHUpdated cache.

    Before #492, five mechanisms wrote it:

    process rate emitter
    P-fixation proxy -0.002 pH per event, one event per layer per day PhosphorusCycle._fix_layer
    NO3-leaching proxy -0.005 pH per event NitrogenCycle._on_water_drained, bottom layer only
    acidifying fertiliser -0.0005 pH per kg/ha nothing in production
    lime (#465) rate / lime requirement, through pHBC apply_lime
    relaxation 0.001 per day toward DayTick.target_ph (6.8) every step_day

    Measured in the #492 refinement (150-day API seasons, urea 40/30/30):

    • Layer-0 pH ended at 6.521287 in all 16 runs. The runs covered sand, loam and clay; 0 to 250 kg N/ha; rain x1, x1.5 and x2; seeds 42 and 43. They agreed to six decimals.
    • Decomposition: -0.300 from the P-fixation proxy and +0.021 from the relaxation. Leaching contributed nothing.
    • The P-fixation proxy is about 1000 times too large. Season P fixation in layer 0 is 0.20 / 0.95 / 2.31 kg P/ha (sand / loam / clay). Even at one H+ per P fixed, that is at most 0.0003 pH. Its sign is doubtful too: phosphate sorption by ligand exchange releases OH-.
    • The leaching proxy counts events, and in the wrong layer. Its event fires only when water leaves the profile bottom, so only the bottom layer acidifies. Rain x2 gave 110 events and -0.275 pH there.

    Over five continuous years (#481 refinement), every soil and climate reached layer-0 pH 5.12, with the subsoil at the 4.0 floor. The equilibrium of the clock against the relaxation is pH 4.8. Grain collapsed through the old microbial pH triangle, which #553 replaced.

    Soil acidification is the cumulative cost of N management. The model modelled it as a clock.

    Decision¶

    (a) pH moves with the proton budget of the N cycle (#492)¶

    NitrogenCycle accrues per-layer signed H+ (kmol/ha) from the transformations it already computes. Stoichiometry per mol N follows Bolan, Hedley & White 1991 (Plant Soil 134:53-63, doi:10.1007/bf00010717) and Helyar & Porter 1989 (in Robson (ed.) Soil Acidity and Plant Growth, pp. 61-101, doi:10.1016/b978-0-12-590655-5.50007-4):

    process where computed H+ per mol N
    urea hydrolysis apply_urea -1
    ammonification (SOM -> NH4+) _on_som_decomposed; _mineralize_layer when self-mineralisation is on -1
    nitrification _nitrify_layer +2
    NO3- uptake _take_up_plant -1
    NH4+ uptake _take_up_plant +1
    denitrification _denitrify_layer -1
    NH3 volatilisation _volatilize_surface (layer 0) +1

    The constants and their reactions live in agrogame/soil/nitrogen/constants.py. Molar mass of N is 14.007 (IUPAC; Meija et al. 2016).

    • No leaching term. The H+ stays in the layer where the nitrate was produced. Nitrate taken up deeper alkalinises that deeper layer. So "wet seasons acidify more" emerges from reduced uptake rather than being added.
    • Ammonium nitrate is a neutral salt. It has no term at application; its NH4+ half acidifies when it nitrifies.

    Release timing. SOM ammonification reaches the N cycle through SOMDecomposed, after NitrogenCycle.daily_step in the same nutrients phase. So NitrogenRuntime releases the day's budget at DayTick(day_end), as one ProtonLoadOccurred(layer, h_kmol_ha) per layer with a non-zero budget.

    Urea is applied between steps, so its hydrolysis term is released at application. Nothing is ever pending at a step boundary. A reset_crop, which rebuilds the N cycle, or a save therefore loses nothing, and the save format is unchanged (ADR-001).

    Conversion. SoilChemistryModule.apply_proton_load converts the load through the #465 buffer capacity:

    dpH = -H+ [kmol/ha] / (pHBC [cmol/kg/pH] x soil mass [kg/ha] / 1e5)

    The result is clamped to [ph_floor, ph_ceiling] = [4.0, 9.0]. Lime uses the same scale: R kg CaCO3 is -R/50 kmol H+.

    Removed. apply_phosphorus_fixation and p_fixation_ph_delta; apply_nutrient_leaching and no3_leach_ph_delta; AcidifyingFertilizerApplied, apply_acidifying_fertilizer and acidifying_ph_delta_per_kg_ha. The chemistry runtime no longer subscribes to NutrientLeached or PhosphorusFixationOccurred. Both events stay, for their own consumers.

    Omitted drivers, bounded:

    • Base-cation removal in harvested product (ash alkalinity): about 0.1-0.2 kmol H+ per t of grain, or -0.004 to -0.008 pH/yr on loam at 4-5 t/ha. That is 10-15% of the N term at 200 kg N. Product coefficients were not verified, so it is left out.
    • Legume N2 fixation: soybean carries a fixed credit with no in-season flux, so there is nothing to attach a term to.
    • Elemental S oxidation (S0 -> SO4 2- + 2 H+): the release schedule exists but is not wired to pH. A follow-up candidate.

    (b) No relaxation and no weathering term (#481 PR A)¶

    The daily relaxation toward 6.8 is an uncited spring. With the budget alone it caps acidification: loam at 200 kg N settles near pH 6.685, a lime dose decays back, and lime never has a job. A relaxation toward a per-soil "equilibrium pH" is the same spring with a different target.

    A mineral-weathering alkalinity flux is citable: critical-loads weathering classes give about +0.001 to +0.006 pH/yr for a 25-30 cm topsoil (#481 refinement, section 3; the weathering-class formula from the UNECE ICP Modelling & Mapping Manual was quoted from memory there and must be checked before any code cites it). But on its own, without the carbonic-acid and bicarbonate-leaching acidity that balances it in real soils, it makes unfertilised pH rise forever. Both fluxes are under 0.01 pH/yr, so both are omitted.

    Implemented by #481 PR A (2026-11-18). SoilChemistryModule.daily_step now only publishes each layer's pH. ChemistryParams.buffering_rate and default_target_ph are deleted. DayTick.target_ph and step_day(target_ph=...) stay for their callers but no longer drive soil pH. Only MicrobesRuntime reads target_ph, and only when no chemistry module is wired. daily_step still accepts a target_ph and ignores it.

    Measured on the #481 5-year harness: loam_temperate, maize, netherlands_temperate, urea 40/30/30, seed 42, the harness in tests/integration/test_ph_no_relaxation.py. Layer-0 pH at each year end:

    run with the relaxation without
    200 kg N 6.771 / 6.741 / 6.723 / 6.710 / 6.702 6.763 / 6.712 / 6.664 / 6.616 / 6.569
    0 kg N 6.800 / 6.800 / 6.800 / 6.800 / 6.800 6.800 / 6.799 / 6.799 / 6.799 / 6.799
    200 kg N + 3 t/ha lime in year 3 year 5: 6.820, 0.119 above unlimed year 5: 6.922, 0.353 above unlimed
    • At 200 kg N the year-4 to year-5 fall grows from 0.008 to 0.047. The spring had brought pH near its asymptote; without it the drift is close to linear.
    • Lime keeps its full step: 3 t/ha of CaCO3 lifts layer 0 by about 0.35 on this loam, and two years later that lift is still there.
    • At 0 kg N, pH still holds within 0.001.

    (c) Per-preset initial pH, calcareous layers, DTPA at the soil's own pH (#481 PR B)¶

    • Presets carry an initial pH in water. The table and its sources are in the #481 refinement: 6.8 for the limed temperate mineral soils, 6.5 for sandy_temperate, 5.8 for peat_cool_wet, 5.5 for sandy_subsaharan (source still to be cited from a table), and 8.0 for sandy_arid.
    • Sources quoting pH(CaCl2) or pH(KCl) are converted (Minasny et al. 2011, Eur. J. Soil Sci. 62:728-732).
    • Calcareous layers are a static flag, invariant under proton loads and lime, with no CaCO3 pool. 2% CaCO3 in a 20 cm topsoil holds about 1300 kmol H+ of capacity, against 6-8 kmol/yr at 250 kg N: more than 150 years. The flag adds no saved state.
    • DTPA micronutrient values are read at the preset's own pH (Lindsay & Norvell 1978).

    (d) clay_netherlands is calcareous (#481 PR B)¶

    It is a young calcareous marine polder clay, initial pH 7.8. It is the one soil where lime is pointless, which is true of Dutch polder clays.

    Microbial activity floor at pH 4.0¶

    553 (PR #555) adopted Rousk, Brookes & Bååth 2009 (*Appl. Environ.¶

    Microbiol. 75:1589-1596): activity is 1.0 at pH 6.8 and above, falls linearly to 2/3 at 4.5, then to 0 at 4.0*.

    The #555 review noted that Rousk et al.'s own data show about 0.18 of maximum at pH 4.0, not 0. This ADR adopts the 0 floor as implemented.

    It is a modelling choice that reads the paper's "all microbial variables were inhibited below pH 4.5" as full inhibition at the chemistry floor (ph_floor = 4.0), not a fit to the data. It does not bind today:

    • With the proton budget no shipped preset approaches pH 4.5 on any game horizon. The fastest drift measured is sand at 300 kg N: -0.11 pH in year 1, about -0.08 pH/yr over five years. The lowest initial pH (c) proposes is 5.5.
    • Between pH 4.5 and 4.0 the adopted line understates activity by at most 0.18 of maximum.

    It must be revisited, toward about 0.18 at pH 4.0, before any preset with an initial pH below 5 ships. One example is the Kenyan acid Nitisol follow-up, at pH 4.07-4.85 (Kisinyo et al. 2013).

    Consequences¶

    • pH follows management. At 0 kg N layer-0 pH holds within 0.001 over a season. At 200 kg N on loam it falls about 0.036 per 150-day season, at 0.47 mol H+ per mol urea-N in layer 0. That is the efficient low end of Goulding 2016 (Soil Use Manage. 32:390-399): 0.56-1.1 mol H+ per mol N over a year, including product removal.
    • Buffering matters. Clay drifts least and sand most. Rain x2 acidifies more, because less nitrate is taken up.
    • Five continuous years (#492 PR evidence, every catalog scenario, seeds 42-46): the clock took every soil to 5.12. Under the budget the drift scales with N rate, and lime persists. Year-5 grain is unchanged or higher in every catalog scenario at typical N.
    • Without the relaxation (#481 PR A evidence, same harness and seeds), year-5 grain matches the relaxation run within 1 g/m² in every catalog scenario. Typical N drifts further: sand 200 kg N reaches 6.31 in year 5, against 6.57. The lowest layer-0 pH in any year and treatment is 5.92 (sand, 300 kg N). The 3 t/ha lime step is about three times what survived the relaxation two years on.
    • At 0 kg N, five-year drift is now visible where drainage is. Year-5 drift is -0.04 in both Kenya scenarios and -0.065 under NL winter wheat, against -0.017 and -0.027 with the relaxation. The temperate maize and Sahel scenarios stay within 0.003 of 6.8.

    The mechanism is nitrate leaching of mineralised native N. By the stoichiometry above, layer 0's net H+ equals (NO3-N drained below it + ΔNO3 - ΔNH4) / 14.007, and the seed-42 ledger closes on that exactly. Over five years, layer 0 drains 215 kg NO3-N under winter wheat and 102 and 157 kg in the two Kenya scenarios, against 11 kg under NL maize.

    The 0.005 bound of #492 applies per 150-day season and still holds. - Single-season baselines are unchanged within 0.3% across the catalog, and the realism suite is unchanged. - Event contract. ProtonLoadOccurred is new: additive in /step payloads, and the client ignores it. AcidifyingFertilizerApplied is gone; nothing ever emitted it. Per-day pH event volume falls. Each layer fixes P most days, and on each such event the old proxy re-published the pH of every layer, about n² SoilPHUpdated events per day. The budget emits one per layer per day, plus one at each urea application. - Ordering for #481. PR A must assert that the P-fixation and leaching proxies no longer exist before it removes the relaxation. Otherwise the -0.30 per season clock runs free: measured 6.07 / 5.34 / 4.61 / 4.00 over four years. PR A asserts it in test_precondition_ph_proxies_are_gone. - #531 (continuous calendar) depends on this ADR. Continuous play under the clock collapsed every soil within 3-5 years. - #558: pH enters nitrification twice. NitrogenCycle._ph_factor, an uncited triangle at 0 below pH 4 and 1 at 7, multiplies the nitrification rate. So does microbial activity, which carries the #553 pH response. Under the budget this double count is now a feedback on the drift itself: lower pH, less nitrification, less acidification. The feedback is stronger than either factor alone. It belongs under this ADR and is fixed in #558, not here.

    Alternatives Considered¶

    • Rescale the P-fixation proxy. It has no basis and its sign is doubtful.
    • Keep a per-kg leaching term. It double-counts under the budget: the acidity of leached nitrate is already in the layer that produced it.
    • Release at the end of NitrogenCycle.daily_step. SOM ammonification arrives after it in the same phase, so a day of protons would carry to the next day. Rejected (#492 AC12).
    • Hold urea hydrolysis until day_end. reset_crop between the application and the step would lose it, and so would a save. Carrying it would need new saved state. Rejected.
    • A CaCO3 pool for calcareous soils. Exact to the flag on any game horizon, at the cost of saved state.
    • A relaxation toward a per-soil equilibrium pH. The same spring: it undoes lime and caps acidification.
